## Purpose
Before generating alternative futures (Imagine), get an honest read on where the mentee actually stands — not a resume, a self-inventory. Skipping this produces Odyssey Plans that are really just guesses dressed up as plans.

## Step 1: The five capitals
Adapted from the Graduate Capital Model (Tomlinson, 2017), originally built for graduate employability but useful as a general self-inventory lens at any career stage. Walk the mentee through each capital with one or two open questions rather than handing them a worksheet to fill in alone — the conversation surfaces things a form doesn't.

- **Human capital** — knowledge and skills. "What can you already do that you'd back yourself on if someone tested it tomorrow?"
- **Social capital** — networks and relationships. "Who would take your call this week if you needed to think something through?"
- **Cultural capital** — fit and self-presentation. "Which rooms do you walk into and immediately feel like you belong? Which ones do you fake it in?"
- **Psychological capital** — resilience under setback. "Think of the last real setback. What did you do in the two weeks after?"
- **Identity capital** — self-concept, the story you tell about your own experience. "If a stranger only had your last five years to go on, what would they conclude you care about?"

Don't score these numerically — that belongs to the Evaluate phase's dashboard, applied to *plans*, not to the person. Here the goal is qualitative: 2-3 honest sentences per capital, logged in `odyssey.md`.

## Step 2: Values, not preferences
Preferences ("I'd like a remote job") are downstream of values ("I value autonomy over my time") and change faster. Anchor on values because they're what the Odyssey Plans in the next phase actually get evaluated against.

Useful prompts (mix question types — see the taxonomy in [mentor-roles.md](mentor-roles.md)):
- "Describe a specific week, real or imagined, that felt completely right. What was actually happening in it?" (open, concrete — avoids abstract values-word lists that people answer aspirationally rather than honestly)
- "What would you regret *not* having tried, five years from now?" (hypothetical)
- "What's a constraint you're not willing to negotiate — money floor, location, time with specific people?" (focused)

Constraints matter as much as values — they're not a smaller version of the same thing. A values list without named constraints produces Odyssey Plans that look great on paper and fail the Resources dial in Evaluate.

## Step 3: True ikigai reflection
Do this **after** capitals and values, and read [ikigai.md](ikigai.md) first — don't reach for the four-circle Venn diagram here; it's not the traditional concept and it over-indexes on career the way this whole Ground phase is trying not to. Instead, ask a small-scale question in the spirit of Ken Mogi's five pillars: "What's something ordinary — not impressive, not resume-worthy — that you'd genuinely miss if it disappeared from your week?" This is diagnostic, not decorative: patterns here (making things with your hands, teaching someone something, solving a puzzle alone vs. with others) often predict what makes an Odyssey Plan *actually* livable better than a job title does.

## Exit criteria
- The mentee has named 2-3 values they're confident in (not guessing), each with a concrete example, not an abstract word
- At least one hard constraint is named explicitly
- The five capitals have a short honest note each, including the weak ones — a Ground phase with only strengths logged didn't do its job
- The mentee has surfaced at least one small daily-life pattern from the ikigai reflection

It does **not** need full self-knowledge — that's what Imagine and Evaluate stress-test. Don't hold Ground to a bar that belongs to later phases.

## Anti-patterns
- **Resume recitation** — mentee lists credentials instead of reflecting. Redirect: "I can see that on a CV already — what I can't see is which of it you'd choose again."
- **Values-as-vocabulary** — abstract words ("growth," "impact") with no concrete instance behind them. Ask for the specific week/moment prompt above until a real example lands.
- **Skipping constraints** — an unconstrained values list. Ask directly what's non-negotiable; don't let this get inferred later when it wrecks a plan's Resources score.
