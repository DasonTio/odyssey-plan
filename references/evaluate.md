## Purpose
Three drafted Odyssey Plans (see [odyssey-plan.md](odyssey-plan.md)) don't rank themselves. Burnett & Evans pair the exercise with a scoring "dashboard," and this phase adds a GROW-style (Whitmore) stress-test on top of it so the choice isn't just a gut-feel score — it gets checked against reality and real alternatives before anyone commits.

## Step 1: The dashboard
For each of the three plans, have the mentee rate four dials 0-100. Don't average them into one number — the pattern across the four matters more than any total, and a low score on one dial is diagnostic, not disqualifying on its own.

- **Resources** — do they have (or can they realistically get) the time, money, skills, and contacts this plan needs?
- **Likability** — independent of practicality, how drawn to this are they?
- **Confidence** — do they believe they could actually pull it off?
- **Coherence** — does this plan fit with who they said they are in Ground (values, constraints, the capitals inventory)?

A plan that scores high on Likability but low on Coherence is worth a direct question: "what does that gap tell you?" Don't resolve it for them.

## Step 2: Stress-test with GROW's Reality and Options
Apply Whitmore's GROW model to whichever plan(s) are scoring well, using its Reality and Options stages specifically (Goal was effectively set in Imagine; Will belongs to Build):

- Reality: "Where are you now in relation to this? What's already been done toward it, even accidentally? What's actually stood in the way so far?"
- Options: "If you'd already done this for a year, what would you have had to do to get there? Who do you know who's already living something like Life Two — what do they actually say about it?"

This is where a plan that looked good on the dashboard alone can fall apart against specifics, or where a plan that scored modestly turns out to have a clear, doable path. Prefer questions that produce information (see the taxonomy in [mentor-roles.md](mentor-roles.md)) over telling the mentee what you think the answer is.

## Step 3: Converge
The chosen direction does not have to be exactly one of the three plans. A common, legitimate outcome is a hybrid — Life One's stability with a Life Three element folded in, or a Life Two contingency kept live alongside a Life One commitment. Ask directly: "if you had to commit to a direction starting Monday, which one — or which combination — are you actually choosing, and why?" The *why* is what gets logged, not just the choice.

## Exit criteria
- All three plans have dashboard scores logged
- At least the leading plan(s) have been through the Reality/Options stress-test, not just scored
- A direction is chosen with a stated reason, referencing specifics from Ground and the dashboard — not just "this one feels right"

## Anti-patterns
- **Scoring as a formality** — numbers assigned fast with no reflection behind them. Slow down on any dial that took less than a few seconds to answer.
- **Ignoring a coherence gap** — high Likability, low Coherence, and the mentee wants to just pick it anyway without addressing the gap. That's their call to make, but name the gap explicitly first; don't let it pass silently (see "Holding a concern under pushback" in [mentor-roles.md](mentor-roles.md)).
- **Premature certainty** — converging to a choice before any Reality/Options questions have been asked of it. Push back once, respect the decision if they still want to proceed.
