## Why this file exists
"Ikigai" gets treated as an ancient Japanese career-optimization framework in almost every career-coaching context it appears in. That claim is false, and repeating it uncritically fails the evidence-driven standard this whole skill is built to. Read this before ever mentioning ikigai to a mentee.

## What most people mean when they say "ikigai"
The four-circle Venn diagram — "what you love," "what you're good at," "what the world needs," "what you can be paid for," with "ikigai" at the center intersection — is what almost everyone pictures. It's a genuinely useful brainstorming lens for surfacing career options. It is not Japanese in origin, and it is not what ikigai means.

## Where that diagram actually came from
- Around 2011, Spanish illustrator/astrologist Andrés Zuzunaga published a Venn diagram about *purpose* — same four circles, no ikigai label.
- In 2014, Marc Winn wrote a blog post that overlaid the Japanese word "ikigai" (borrowed from Dan Buettner's Blue Zones longevity research) onto Zuzunaga's Purpose diagram, replacing the center label. That mashup is the diagram now everywhere.
- The result spread through HR and career-coaching content as if it were traditional Japanese wisdom. It isn't — it's a five-year-old Western remix wearing a borrowed word.

## What ikigai actually means in Japanese use
Ikigai translates roughly to "reason for being" or "the thing that gets you out of bed in the morning" — and in ordinary Japanese usage it is *not* primarily about career at all. Neuroscientist Ken Mogi, writing specifically on this, proposes five pillars: starting small, accepting yourself, harmony and sustainability, the joy of little things, and being in the here and now. His own example of ikigai is enjoying a cup of coffee in the morning. It's found more often in gardening, cooking, a specific relationship, or a small daily ritual than in a job title — and critically, "what you can be paid for" is not part of the traditional concept at all.

## How to use both, honestly, in this skill
- Never present the four-circle Venn as "the ancient Japanese ikigai framework." If you use it (it's a fine brainstorming tool), call it what it is: a 2011–2014 Western purpose diagram that later borrowed the word ikigai.
- Use the *authentic* concept in [intake.md](intake.md)'s Ground phase — the small-daily-thing question — because it's diagnostic in a way the career Venn isn't: it surfaces what actually sustains someone day to day, independent of any job title, and that's what makes an Odyssey Plan (see [odyssey-plan.md](odyssey-plan.md)) livable rather than just aspirational.
- If a mentee explicitly asks for "the ikigai diagram," give it to them — but correct the record first in one or two sentences, then proceed. Don't lecture; don't withhold something they asked for because the popular version is technically wrong.
